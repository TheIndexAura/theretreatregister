How the register is built

  1. We scout and filter

    We search a market town by town and shelve what does not meet the standard.

  2. We check against the venue itself

    Software reads the venue’s own website, not a listing about the venue. Where something cannot be confirmed, the listing says so.

  3. We show the receipts

    Coverage snapshots, findings from the data, and the cuts. On July 11, 2026 a person read every published row, every name and every website, and cut 274 entries the software had missed.2

  4. Owners take the wheel

    Claiming is free for the first 60 days after the register opens. We hand the keys back to venue owners and invite them to claim, polish, and perfect their listings.

What this is

A register, not a broker

Finding a venue for a company offsite still takes weeks. Search results bury pricing behind quote forms and cluster around the same ten famous destinations. Planners end up cross-checking every listing against the venue’s own website anyway, because the listing cannot be trusted on its own.


The Retreat Register works the other way around. It is a reference work. Venues are cataloged from public sources, then checked against their own websites, and the ones that do not belong are cut and counted in public. Budget chains, convention halls, and dead listings are filtered out. A venue is either good enough to be in the register, or it is not in it.


The Retreat Register screens for essentials before listing a venue, focusing first on a direct phone number and dedicated meeting or lodging space.

The difference

What "curated" means here

  • A listing never shows a different property. Until an owner claims their listing and uploads their own photographs, the register matches the venue with a close stand-in image, and the image is treated as what it is.
  • Straight answers on price. Most venues quote per group, because head count, season, and whether you take the whole property all move the number. That is how this industry works, and the register measured how far it goes: on July 12, 2026, 133 of the 1,374 venues on file published a starting price, which is 9.7%.5 Five days later, with 2,281 venues on file, 188 published one, which is 8.2%.7 Where public pricing exists, the register shows it. Where it does not, the listing says “quote only” instead of printing a guess.
  • The whole map, not the famous ten. Aspen and Napa are here, and so are Mendocino, Taos, Leavenworth, and Amelia Island. On July 12, 2026 the twenty deepest states held 715 of the register’s 1,374 venues, which is 52% of it, and the other 659 were spread across the remaining thirty states and the District of Columbia.8 Coverage is top-heavy, and the small markets are where the register does the work nobody else bothers with.
  • The cuts are published too. On July 11, 2026 a person read every published row and cut 274 entries.2 On July 12, 2026 the register put 2,811 of its own past rejections back on trial: 606 were restored, 2,193 rejections were upheld, and 12 were set aside as obvious junk.6 A register that only adds is a list. A register that also corrects itself is a reference work.

From the data

The number that does not move

Between July 12 and July 18, 2026 the register grew from 1,374 venues to 2,281, and was then cut back to 2,125.874 Across every one of those readings, the share of venues carrying a direct phone number stayed within half a point of 91%.


A ratio that holds steady while the sample nearly doubles is telling you something about the industry rather than about the sample. Nine venues in ten will pick up the phone. Roughly one in eleven will tell you a price before you ask.

Questions, answered plainly

FAQ

What is The Retreat Register?
A national directory of corporate retreat venues in the United States: resorts, lodges, ranches, estates, and purpose-built conference retreats. It is free to browse. It launches in 2026, and venues nationwide are already cataloged.
How are venues chosen?
Venues are cataloged from public sources, then filtered hard. A listing has to be a real property with real lodging or dedicated meeting space, and software checks that against the venue’s own website. Budget hotel chains, municipal convention centers, and expo halls are kept out. Properties that fail get cut rather than published with a caveat: on July 11, 2026 a person read every published row and cut 274 entries the software had missed.2 The methodology page walks through the whole process.
What does a corporate retreat venue cost?
Most venues quote per group, because head count, season, and whether you take the whole property all move the number. A published starting price is rare across the industry. On July 12, 2026, 133 of the register’s 1,374 venues published one, which is 9.7%.5 Where a venue publishes pricing, the register shows it. Where it is quote-only, the listing says so plainly instead of guessing.
Can church groups and congregations use it?
Yes. Faith-based retreat centers are included when they meet the same quality bar as every other venue: real retreat lodging, attractive grounds, a professional operation, and a genuine welcome for outside groups. Congregation getaways and men's, women's, and youth retreats are a core use.
What kinds of venues are listed?
Resorts, lodges, ranches, estates, boutique hotels with dedicated meeting space, casino lodges, and purpose-built conference retreat campuses. Not listed: municipal convention centers, expo halls, banquet-only facilities, and budget or economy chains.
What does claiming a listing give a venue owner?
Claiming is free for the first 60 days after the register opens. It lets an owner add details, replace a stand-in image with their own photographs, and put current contact information in front of planners. That is why the register looks for the basics first, like a direct phone line and physical event space. Claiming your venue profile ensures corporate planners connect straight to your team without any missing details.
How current are the numbers on this site?
Every figure carries the date it was measured, and older figures are left up rather than quietly refreshed. The most recent exact total published is 2,152 curated venues on July 19, 2026.1 Where a number has not been measured, the site does not print one.
