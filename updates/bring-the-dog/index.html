Bring the dog: pet policy questions for planners

August 12, 2026 · The Retreat Register

Someone you need at the retreat just said no, and the reason is their dog. They have no sitter they trust and no kennel they like, so the dog comes or they stay home. That one small detail can shrink a gathering before the invitations are even answered.

A clear pet policy can turn that no into a yes. For plenty of people a dog is family, so a trip that leaves it home is a trip they skip.

A pet fee says little about how pet-friendly a venue is. Ground-floor rooms with direct access to the outdoors, or a fenced area where a dog can run, say more. Those details show that someone thought about a guest traveling with an animal. An old policy can shrink your options without anyone saying so.

Start with venues that list any pet accommodation. Write to each one about room type, outdoor access and exercise areas, and get every fee and rule in writing. Today, email the venues on your short list and ask for the full pet policy. The replies will show whether the whole team, dogs included, can fit.

Service dogs and pets are two different questions

Federal law treats them separately. Under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), a service animal is a dog trained to do specific work for a person with a disability, and the Department of Justice FAQ says comfort and emotional support animals do not qualify. A venue with a no-pets rule may still have to admit a service dog. For an emotional support dog, the same FAQ notes that some state or local laws allow it, so the answer changes by place. Sort your team's needs into these two groups first.

What the rules say about rooms and fees

The FAQ also speaks to lodging. A hotel may not limit a guest with a service animal to pet-friendly rooms, and it may not charge a cleaning fee for hair or dander. It can bill the usual rate for damage. The handler is responsible for the animal's care, and the venue does not have to supervise it. Staff who are unsure may ask only two questions, and they may not ask for papers, as that guidance explains.

What to ask a venue about pets in writing

A pet policy is the venue's own rule, so the details vary. Ask which rooms take dogs and whether those rooms can be held for your group. Then ask what the fee covers, who pays for damage, and where dogs may go once inside, such as the dining room. Keep the two lists apart. The Justice Department's service animal requirements page calls service animals "working animals, not pets," and a guest with one should never be sent to a pet room by mistake.
