How Far Ahead to Book a Retreat: How Planners Set the Booking Date

August 26, 2026 · The Retreat Register

There is no single right time to book a retreat. Season, group size and how much of the property you need all move the date, and flexible dates win some of it back. Work backward from the retreat, ask venues what is already held, and check the holiday and storm calendars before you sign.

Know what moves the booking date

Ask five planners how far ahead to book a retreat and you will hear five answers. Each answer comes from a different kind of trip. A small team that can meet midweek in the off season shops from a wide choice of venues and may be able to book late. A large group that needs a whole property on a popular fall weekend competes with weddings, reunions and other companies for the same dates.

Three things move your date more than any rule of thumb. Season comes first, along with how many other groups want it. Size matters next, because fewer venues can take forty people than twelve. Then there is how much of the property you need. A few rooms in a large lodge are easier to find than an exclusive buyout of a small one.

Count beds as well as people. A group of twenty that needs single rooms is a bigger booking than a group of twenty happy to share. Couples, shared rooms and guests who live close enough to sleep at home all change the number of beds you need, and that number decides which venues can take you at all.

Flexibility works in your favor. A group that can shift by a week, or accept a Tuesday to Thursday instead of a weekend, sees more open dates. Settle how fixed your dates really are before you look at a single venue.

Work backward from the retreat

A booking date is the result of several earlier steps, and each step takes time. Start from the retreat and count back. List the decisions that have to happen first and who has to make them.

Before you can sign a contract, you need a headcount you trust, a budget someone has approved and a short list of venues that fit. A short list, in turn, depends on replies about dates, rooms and meeting space. If a site visit matters to your group, add time for that, and for the follow-up email that confirms what you saw.

After you sign, there is still work. Expect a request for the rooming list, meal choices and dietary needs some weeks out. Guests need travel details early enough to book flights at sensible times. Ask each venue for its own deadlines for those, and put them on your calendar the day you sign.

Add up the time each step takes for your organization, honestly, including the week the approver is on holiday. That total, counted back from the retreat, tells you when to start asking venues.

Here is how that looks in practice. Say your approver needs two weeks to sign off a budget, venues take a week to reply, and a site visit needs another two weeks to arrange. Add a week to read the contract and a week for the deposit to clear. Seven weeks, and nothing has started yet. Do the same sum with your own numbers before the first email goes out.

Check the holiday calendar before you choose a weekend

Holiday weekends are worth checking first. The Office of Personnel Management publishes the list of federal holidays each year, and from late summer through the fall it includes Labor Day, Columbus Day, Veterans Day and Thanksgiving Day.1 Ask venues whether the long weekends around those dates are popular with families and other groups.

Look up the holiday dates for your year on that list before you pick a weekend. Then ask each venue whether those weekends are already held, and ask about a version of your retreat a week earlier or later.

Check your own team's calendar, too. School holidays, the end of a financial quarter or a busy product season can make a date hard for your people even when the venue is free. Ask a few people from different parts of the team before you commit.

Ask each venue about local events as well. A town festival, a college graduation weekend or a road race can fill every room nearby and close roads on the day your group arrives. A venue will know its busy weekends. Ask it to name them.

Plan for storms on coastal dates from June through November

Retreat dates near the coast in summer and fall sit inside the Atlantic hurricane season. The National Weather Service says the season runs from the first of June through the end of November.2 It lists inland flooding from heavy rains among the main hazards,2 so the question applies to some venues away from the beach as well.

The booking date matters here because the storm terms are part of the contract. Before you sign for a coastal venue in those months, ask in writing what happens if a storm forces a change of dates or an evacuation. Will the venue move you to new dates, return the deposit or hold it as a credit? Ask how the contract handles an official evacuation order or closure, who tells the group, and what happens to the booking. Get the answer into the contract as well as the email.

If your dates are fixed and the storm terms are weak, weigh a venue away from the coast for those months. If your dates can move, ask whether the venue will hold a second set of dates for you at no charge.

Ask venues the questions that set your timeline

The fastest way to learn your real booking date is to ask the venues directly, in writing, early. Send your short list the same email with your group size, the rooms you need, the meeting space you need and two or three possible date ranges.

Ask each venue which of those dates are open, which are already held by another group, and how long it will hold a date for you while you get approval. Ask when it needs a signed contract and a deposit to keep the date. Ask what the cancellation terms are at each stage, so you know what a late change in headcount would cost.

Ask how holds work, too. Some venues keep a first hold for one group and a second hold for the next, and release the date if the first group does not sign by a set day. Find out where you stand on each date and when that day falls. A hold you forget about can expire while your budget is still waiting for a signature.

Compare the replies side by side. The dates that are open at several venues give you more choices. The dates that are held almost everywhere tell you to move or to book quickly.

Here is a sentence you can paste into your first message: "Please tell me which of the date ranges below are open for a group of this size, how long you can hold a date while the group gets approval, and when you need a signed contract and deposit."
