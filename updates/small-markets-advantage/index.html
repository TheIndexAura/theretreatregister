Try a small-market venue for your next retreat

July 17, 2026 · The Retreat Register

Aspen, Miami and Napa top plenty of shortlists. A town with fewer visitors can leave more dates open and give your group more of the venue's attention.

Put one marquee name and one small market on your shortlist and compare them side by side.

A Louisiana lodge can fit your team as well as one in California or New York. Pricing is quote-only by default, so you are asking either way. The direct phone test explains what a listed number tells you, and email makes a good first contact.

Check how your group gets there

A small town changes the travel plan first. The Federal Aviation Administration sorts commercial airports by traffic. A primary nonhub airport handles more than 10,000 but less than 0.05 percent of annual U.S. commercial boardings. A nonprimary commercial airport sees between 2,500 and 10,000 boardings a year, per the FAA airport categories page.

At the low end of the FAA categories, 2,500 boardings a year averages under seven people a day. Twelve people boarding together is more than the average day at an airport on that line. Ask the venue in writing which airport guests use and how long the drive runs. Then price the flights yourself.

What small actually means

The USDA Economic Research Service defines rural towns as places with fewer than 5,000 people and 2,000 housing units. It also says nonmetro counties include urban areas of up to 50,000 people. Your small market could be either. See the ERS rural classifications page for the full definitions.

That range, set out by the Economic Research Service, is wide, so a label like small market tells you little until you know the population. Look up the town. Then ask the venue what else nearby has rooms and meals for your group. Today, pick one marquee name and one small market from your shortlist and ask both for the nearest airport and the drive time.
