Glamping or Cabins? Choose the Right Lodging for Your Group Retreat

October 7, 2026 · The Retreat Register

The planner scrolls through photos of canvas tents on wooden platforms and books the site for a spring retreat. The first night drops to forty degrees and the group wakes shivering in sleeping bags while the organizer stares at a portable heater that trips the breaker.

That moment is where the comparison between glamping vs cabins for groups stops being theoretical and starts being practical. The photo sold the tent but the weather revealed what the listing did not say about heat and power and bathroom access.

How to compare heating and cooling

Start by asking the venue for the heating method in each unit and the maximum output in British thermal units. A propane wall heater in a canvas tent behaves differently from a mini split in an insulated cabin when the temperature falls below freezing. Ask for the date the system was last serviced and whether the fuel source is included in the rate or billed separately.

Request the cooling specifications for summer dates. A tent with only roof vents and a battery fan will not hold a comfortable temperature for a work session when the afternoon sun hits the fabric. A cabin with shaded porches and cross ventilation may stay ten degrees cooler without mechanical cooling.

Write the specifications into a simple spreadsheet with one column for each unit type and one row for each performance metric. This sheet becomes the reference you send to the venue with follow up questions in writing.

How to verify bathroom arrangements

Confirm whether each unit has a private toilet and shower or whether the group shares a central bathhouse. A shared bathhouse means morning queues that can delay the first session by thirty minutes or more. Count the number of stalls and showers per guest and compare that ratio to the group schedule.

Ask for the distance from the farthest sleeping unit to the bathhouse in feet and whether the path is lit and paved. A two hundred foot walk in rain or dark changes the calculus for attendees with mobility limits. Request photos of the interior taken in the last six months so you can see the condition of fixtures and ventilation.

Note the hot water capacity in gallons and the recovery time. A tankless system sized for two cabins will not support eight tents running showers at the same time. Put the capacity numbers in the same spreadsheet so the comparison stays side by side.

How to assess noise and power

Canvas walls transmit sound from neighboring units and from the surrounding environment. Ask the venue for the typical decibel reading inside a unit when the adjacent unit is occupied and talking at normal volume. Request the quiet hours policy in writing and the enforcement procedure.

Power for laptops and projectors requires more than a single outlet per tent. Ask for the amperage of the circuit serving each unit and whether the circuit is dedicated or shared with lighting and heating. A fifteen amp circuit shared with a space heater will not support a charging station for twelve devices.

Confirm the backup power plan. A property with a whole site generator that switches over in thirty seconds keeps the work session running. A property with no backup means the agenda stops when the grid fails. Record the generator capacity in kilowatts and the fuel supply duration in hours.

How to confirm accessibility and season length

Measure the door width and threshold height for each unit type and compare them to the needs of any attendee who uses a wheelchair or walker. Ask for photos of the path from the parking area to the unit showing surface material and grade. A gravel path with a six percent slope is not accessible even if the unit interior meets code.

Request the operating season dates for each unit type. Some canvas platforms close from November through March while the cabins on the same property remain open. If the retreat dates fall near the edge of the season confirm that the heating system is rated for the expected low temperature.

Ask whether the venue provides a written accessibility statement that includes bathroom grab bars, shower seats, and clearance dimensions. A statement prepared by the property owner carries more weight than a verbal assurance during a site visit.

How to evaluate meeting space

Identify the indoor space that can accommodate the full group with tables and chairs in a classroom layout. Request the square footage and the ceiling height. A tent with a center pole and a twelve foot peak may only offer eight feet of usable height at the edges where the projector screen sits.

Ask for the heating and cooling capacity of the meeting space separate from the sleeping units. A space that relies on the same propane heater as the adjacent tents will not maintain a steady temperature when the door opens for breaks. Confirm the number of dedicated circuits and the location of outlets relative to the presenter position.

Verify the availability of a hardwired internet connection in the meeting space. Wireless signal in a canvas structure often drops when the group size exceeds twenty devices. Request a speed test result taken during a previous event of similar size and note the upload and download numbers in your spreadsheet.

You must confirm the propane tank run time at full output and whether a spare tank sits on site. The tank sits outside the unit and the gauge freezes at forty degrees so you cannot read it in the dark. This affects the morning heat reliability. Check the gauge daily.

Calculate the peak demand by multiplying guests by shower minutes and dividing by stalls. The shower drain clogs with hair by day two and the maintenance staff arrives at noon. This determines the morning queue length. Plan for the clog.

Request the generator decibel rating at twenty feet and compare it to the quiet hours threshold. The generator exhaust often points at the tent row and the sound carries over the gravel path. This determines the sleep quality for light sleepers. Walk the path at night.

Measure the path grade yourself with a level and a straight board if the venue has no certificate. The gravel shifts after rain and the six percent slope becomes eight percent at the edge. This determines independent access for wheelchair users. Measure after rain.

Test the internet with a video call at session time using a hotspot from another carrier. The local tower serves a weekend market and the signal drops when the crowd arrives. This determines the backup connectivity plan. Test on Saturday.

Position the projector so the throw distance matches the screen width minus the center pole obstruction. The center pole often sits where the presenter stands and the lens shift range cannot compensate. This determines the screen placement for readable text. Check the lens shift range.

The task is to build a verification spreadsheet that covers heat, bathrooms, noise, power, accessibility, season, and meeting space for each unit type under consideration. Send the spreadsheet to the venue with a written request for the specifications and the most recent photos of each item. Pricing in this industry is quote only by default so the spreadsheet also serves as the basis for a written quote request.

When the venue replies with the completed data you can compare glamping vs cabins for groups on the factors that actually determine comfort and productivity. A direct phone number and a dedicated meeting or lodging space are the two essentials that most often decide whether a retreat venue works for a group so prioritize those confirmations first. The next step is to review the returned spreadsheet and eliminate any option that does not meet the minimum thresholds you set before the first deposit is due.
