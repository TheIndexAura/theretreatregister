"Do You Have Wi-Fi?" Is Not Enough for a Company Retreat

August 8, 2026 · The Retreat Register

"Is there Wi-Fi?" It's the simplest question a retreat planner asks, and the answer is always "yes." A confident nod, a quick affirmation. But that "yes" can be the villain of your next offsite.

We've seen it too many times: Monday morning, everyone logs on for the working session, and the whole operation grinds to a halt. Buffering screens, dropped calls, and frustrated faces. The promise of productivity vanishes like smoke.

The problem isn't malice; it's a difference in definition. A venue might have Wi-Fi for checking email in the main lodge, but not for twenty people streaming high-definition video from separate rooms. The Retreat Register screens for essentials before listing a venue, checking things like direct phone reachability and real lodging or meeting space. But Wi-Fi capacity is a trickier beast. Asking "what happens when our whole group is on video calls at once?" changes the conversation from a simple yes to a practical assessment.

You also need to ask where that signal actually reaches. Does it extend to the quiet cabins where people might want to work solo? Is the barn, earmarked for your creative breakout, a digital dead zone? Decide on purpose which parts of your retreat need robust connection and which can thrive without it. Here's the heresy: dead zones aren't always a defect. The best retreats use them on purpose, creating space for true disconnection and focused interaction.

Don't just ask if there's Wi-Fi; ask how much and where. Plan your digital landscape with intention, embracing the quiet corners as much as the connected hubs. This approach turns a potential problem into a strategic asset, ensuring your team gets the right kind of connection – or glorious disconnection – exactly when they need it.
