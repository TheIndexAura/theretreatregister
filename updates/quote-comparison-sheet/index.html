Compare Retreat Venue Quotes on One Sheet: Rows, Fees and Totals

August 21, 2026 · The Retreat Register

Quotes from different retreat venues rarely match. One prices each guest, one gives a flat figure for the whole property, and one leaves meals out. Build a comparison sheet before the second reply arrives, put every charge on its own row, and send each blank back to the venue as a written question.

Why quotes do not line up

Ask three venues for a quote and you may get three different kinds of document. The first prices each guest for each night. The second gives one flat figure for the whole property. The third looks cheapest because it leaves meals out without saying so. Placed side by side from memory, they cannot be compared, and choosing between them turns into guessing.

The fix is a single sheet that every quote gets rewritten into. Do it as each reply lands, not at the end. Early on you still have room to ask follow-up questions.

Give the sheet one row each for lodging, meals, meeting space, service fees, taxes, the deposit and the cancellation terms. Add a row for anything else the venue mentions, such as equipment, cleaning or a charge for extra guests. Then give each venue a column.

When a quote arrives, translate it into those rows in your own words. Where the venue is silent on a row, write a plain mark in the cell and keep going. Those blanks are the most useful part of the sheet. A blank row is a question you have not asked yet, and until the venue fills it, the comparison is not real. Send the questions back in one email and ask for the answers in writing, so each blank gets a written reply before you compare, because a figure you cannot see in a reply is not yet a figure.

Two rows tend to hide differences, so define them before you compare. For meals, write down what the quote covers: which meals, whether drinks and breaks are included and whether service is part of the price. For meeting space, note which room, for how many hours a day, and whether screens, chairs and setup are included or billed on top. A package and an itemized bill may arrive at the same total through very different routes.

Keep the units the same across venues. If one quote is per person and another is for the whole group, convert both into the same unit before you compare, and write the conversion in a note so you can show your work later. A comparison nobody can check is only an opinion.

Give taxes and mandatory fees their own rows

Add two columns to the sheet. The first asks whether a charge is mandatory. The second asks whether it sits inside the quoted total. Federal fee rules give you the logic to borrow. The federal rule on unfair or deceptive fees defines a total price as the maximum total of all fees or charges a consumer must pay, including any mandatory ancillary good or service.1 The same definition allows government charges to be left out.1

That gives you a test for your sheet. Keep three figures apart: the quoted total, the charges left outside it and the final amount you would pay. A mandatory service fee that sits outside the total deserves a note and a question. Ask the venue why it is separate and whether it is refundable, and put the answer in the cell.

Give optional extras their own block at the bottom of the sheet. The same definition lets charges for an optional ancillary good or service stay out of the total, so a late checkout, an upgraded meal plan or an extra shuttle can sit apart.1 Ask each venue to label every line in its reply as required or optional. Labeled lines make your sheet easier to fill, and they show how the venue handles detail.

Be careful about what this rule is. It covers consumer prices for short-term lodging and live-event tickets, and it speaks of consumers.1 A group retreat contract may or may not fall under it. Use it as a yardstick for wording and structure, and draw no legal conclusion from it. If a question about a specific fee matters to your decision, ask a lawyer who handles contracts.

Compare the all-in totals

The rule says that a business showing a price must disclose the total price more prominently than any other pricing information.2 Borrow that habit for your own sheet. Put the quoted total and the final amount you would pay in the top rows of each column, and push the nightly or per-person rate below them. Have each venue confirm which figure is the full amount the group would pay.

The rule also requires a business to disclose, before the buyer agrees to pay, the nature, purpose and amount of any charge excluded from the total, along with the final amount of payment.2 You can ask the same of a venue. Request a line for every excluded charge, saying what it is for, how much it is and which part of the stay it applies to.

A quote that leads with a low rate and shows the total further down makes your comparison harder. One that leads with the total, lists what is outside it and explains each line makes it easier. Consider which quote you could check line by line.

The rule also treats it as unfair and deceptive to misrepresent the nature, purpose, amount or refundability of a fee, or the good or service the fee is charged for.3 Those four details make a handy checklist for each charge on your sheet. What is it called? What does it pay for? How much is it? Can any of it come back?

A line that says resort fee tells you very little. A line that says use of the fitness room, the shuttle and the meeting-room screen, required for all guests and not refundable, tells you what you are paying for. If a charge is vague, ask the venue to rewrite it in the same email where it answers your other blanks.

Write the expiry date on each quote

Add a row for how long each quote holds. Find out whether prices can change between replies, and get the date confirmed. If the date falls before you can decide, ask whether the venue will extend it or hold the dates for you, and what that hold requires from you.

Add one more row for what happens to the deposit. Find out when it is due, whether any part of it is refundable and what the cancellation terms say. These details can matter as much as a small gap in the totals, and they are easy to lose when a quote runs long.

Once every venue has filled every row, read across the sheet, circle the totals and pick the two best. Then send each one a last short message with only the remaining questions. Compare the replies to that message line by line, and note any row that is still blank. Keep the finished sheet with the signed contract, so that anyone who asks later why you chose this venue can see the comparison behind the decision.
