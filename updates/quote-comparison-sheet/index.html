Planner Guide

Compare venue quotes on one sheet before the second reply

August 21, 2026 · The Retreat Register

The first quote is per person per night. The second is one flat number for the whole property. The third looks cheapest because it quietly leaves meals out.

Side by side, they do not line up, and choosing from memory means guessing.

Build the sheet before the second reply lands. Give it one row each for lodging, meals, meeting space, service fees, taxes, deposit, and cancellation terms. When a quote arrives, rewrite it into those rows and mark every blank the venue left.

Those blanks are the useful part. A missing row is a question to send back in writing, and until the venue answers it, the comparison is not real.

Give taxes and mandatory fees their own rows

Add two columns to the sheet: is this charge mandatory, and is it inside the quoted total? Federal fee rules give the logic. Under 16 CFR Part 464, a total price covers the stay plus any mandatory extra, and government charges may be left out. So a tax line outside the total is normal, but a mandatory service fee outside it needs a note.

Sort optional from required

Give optional extras their own block on the sheet. The rule lets them stay outside the total price, so a late checkout or an upgraded meal plan can sit apart. Ask each venue to label every line as required or optional in its reply. The rule text is a useful model for the wording. It applies to consumer prices, so use it as a yardstick for a group quote and draw no legal conclusion from it.

Compare the all-in totals

The fee rule says a seller must show the total price more prominently than any other price. Do the same on your sheet. Put the all-in total in the top row and push the nightly rate below it. A venue that leads with the rate and buries the total has told you something about how the invoice may read.

Write the expiry date on each quote

Prices move. In the Amex GBT 2026 global meetings and events forecast, seven in ten meeting professionals (71%) expected cost increases in the year ahead. Add a row for how long each quote holds, and ask for the date in writing.
