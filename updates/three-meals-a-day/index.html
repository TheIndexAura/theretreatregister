Your Most Productive Retreat Session May Not Be on the Agenda

August 14, 2026 · The Retreat Register

Many retreat planners spend hours on workshop schedules. They fine-tune every minute of instruction and activity.

Often, meals become mere fuel stops in the agenda. Planners then wonder why the group did not quite connect as hoped.

Connection often blossoms when the schedule loosens, particularly around the table. The best moments at any retreat frequently happen during meals, lingering past the planned hour. This is where shared stories and genuine bonds form.

Consider the dining setup as carefully as any session plan. One long table often encourages group interaction more effectively than several scattered four-tops. An unhurried dinner allows for deeper conversation, unlike a rushed, efficient meal. Remember, pricing in this industry is quote-only by default, so specific inquiries are necessary. The venue's default meal setup should be a selection criterion, alongside a direct phone number and a dedicated meeting or lodging space, which are the two essentials that most often decide whether a retreat venue works for a group.

Skill: Designing for Table Connection. When evaluating venues, inquire about their standard dining configurations. Request a setup with one long table if possible, and build unhurried time into your meal agenda. Today, draft an email inquiry to potential venues asking specific questions about their dining room layouts and their flexibility regarding meal pacing for groups.
