Planner Guide

Write one venue email that gets comparable replies

August 20, 2026 · The Retreat Register

Write to five venues in five different ways and you get five replies that refuse to line up. Most searches begin like that, with a fresh note typed for each place.

Write one email instead, once, and send the same text to every venue on the list.

Number the questions so each venue answers them in order: which dates hold, how many people sleep where, the all-in quote with fees included, how meals run, and what the deposit and cancellation terms say. With a numbered list, a skipped answer shows up as a gap with a number on it.

Keep every exchange in writing. An email thread settles a later disagreement in minutes. How fast and how completely each venue answers tells you something too. Then lay the replies side by side, and the comparison is already built.

Ask for one total, then the exceptions

Ask for the price as a single number with every mandatory charge inside it. Federal fee rules for lodging use the same idea. In 16 CFR Part 464, a total price is the most a buyer must pay for the stay and any mandatory extra, while government charges and optional extras may sit outside it. Borrow that split. Put taxes on their own line and ask which other charges are mandatory.

The rule is written around prices shown to consumers, so a group contract may fall outside it, though its definitions still make a fair template for your question.

Ask what every other fee is for

Add a numbered question for every charge outside the quote: what is it for, and what does it cost? The same rule requires a seller to disclose any fee left out of the total price before the buyer agrees to pay, along with what it is for and how much it is (see section 464.2). Ask for the answer in the reply, so it sits in the same thread as the quote.

Ask how access is described

Add one numbered question about accessibility. The ADA National Network factsheet on accessible lodging says covered hotels, motels and inns must describe their accessible features in enough detail for a guest to decide whether the place fits. Ask for that description of guest rooms and meeting space in writing. An owner-occupied place with five rooms or fewer is exempt from those rules, so expect a plainer answer there.
