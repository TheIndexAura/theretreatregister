Planner Guide

Ask who runs the kitchen before you book a retreat

August 14, 2026 · The Retreat Register

Every venue menu reads like poetry. Braised this, locally foraged that. Then Saturday arrives, and the meal is whoever happens to be standing at the stove. The cook's name is the question nobody asks.

Ask who runs the kitchen and how long they have been running it. Then ask how a dietary need travels from your spreadsheet to the person holding the pan.

A kitchen with an owner answers in a sentence. A kitchen without one answers in logistics. Either way you learn what you are booking: three meals a day of it.

Meals happen more often than sessions do, so the cook shapes more of your retreat than the agenda does.

Use the federal allergen list as your form

US law names nine major food allergens: milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans and sesame (FDA, Food Allergies). Build your dietary intake form from that list and send the same nine lines to the kitchen. Guests then answer in categories the cook already knows. The FDA also notes that allergic reactions range from hives to anaphylaxis, which can involve fatal breathing problems, and that food allergies cannot be cured.

Ask about cross-contact, by name

The FDA uses the term cross-contact for the accidental introduction of a major allergen into a food (FDA, Food Allergies). Ask the person who runs the kitchen how that is prevented on a night with thirty plates going out. A cook with a routine can usually describe it in a few lines. A cook without one may talk about the menu instead. Either reply tells you something, and you want it in writing before you sign.

Ask which rules the kitchen cooks under

The FDA publishes a Food Code, a model that local, state, tribal and federal regulators use to write or update their own rules for restaurants, grocery stores and institutions (FDA, FDA Food Code). Because each jurisdiction adopts its own version, the rules in your venue's county may not match the rules in the last county you booked. Ask for the name of the agency that inspects the kitchen and the date of its latest inspection. Then ask, in the same email, who is on shift that Saturday.
