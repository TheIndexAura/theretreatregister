Shared Rooms or Singles? Decide Before You Shortlist

September 4, 2026 · The Retreat Register

When a group retreats, the question of who sleeps where often appears late in the planning cycle. The surprise of discovering that the only available dormitories consist of bunkbeds, or that a budget collapses because private rooms were assumed, is a predictable source of friction. The root of the problem is a shortlist created before any discussion of retreat room assignments shared rooms. By fixing the sleeping arrangement rule before the venue search, the later scramble disappears.

The remedy is straightforward: decide the sharing policy, capture every participant’s preference in writing, and then compare those data points with the floor plans of potential sites. The following guide walks the planner through each of those steps, citing the two pieces of information that most often decide whether a venue will work: a direct phone number and a dedicated meeting or lodging space. The result is a shortlist that already respects the agreed‑upon sleeping structure.

Set a clear sharing policy

The first task is to name the policy decision: who may share a room, who must have a private space, and what cost the organization will cover for extra beds. Begin by listing categories such as single adults, couples, families with children, and participants with accessibility needs. For each category, note whether sharing is permitted and whether the organization will pay for a second mattress or a separate room. Document the outcome in a short memo that can be referenced when evaluating venues.

Next, define a baseline check for budget impact. Calculate the per‑person cost for a private room versus a shared arrangement, using the quote‑only pricing model that dominates the industry. Multiply the difference by the number of participants who will require private space under the policy. This arithmetic reveals whether the current budget can sustain the chosen sharing rule.

Finally, circulate the policy memo to the planning committee for written acknowledgement. Ask each member to confirm that the policy aligns with the group’s expectations and that no hidden assumptions remain. The written record prevents later disputes and provides a reference point when negotiating with venues.

Gather sleeping preferences in writing

After the policy is set, the next task is to collect individual preferences. Deploy a simple email questionnaire that asks for preferred sleeping arrangement, any medical or accessibility requirements, and willingness to share a room with a stranger. Require a response by a fixed deadline so that the data set is complete before venue outreach begins.

Compile the responses into a spreadsheet that tags each participant by the categories defined in the policy memo. Use a column to mark “must have private” and another for “open to shared”. This visual map makes it easy to count how many private rooms are mandatory and how many shared rooms can be allocated.

Once the spreadsheet is finalized, share it with the planning team and ask for a written sign‑off that the preferences have been captured accurately. The sign‑off serves as the final checkpoint before any venue is considered, helping to keep the next phase from overlooking any request.

Match policy to venue layouts

With the policy and preferences locked, the venue search can begin. Request a floor plan and a quote from each potential site, remembering that pricing in this industry is quote‑only by default. Ask the venue to confirm the presence of a direct phone number and a dedicated meeting or lodging space, the two essentials that most often decide whether a retreat venue works for a group.

Compare the floor plan against the spreadsheet of preferences. Count the number of private rooms, double‑occupancy rooms, and bunkroom configurations. If the venue offers fewer private rooms than the number required by the policy, discard it from the shortlist. Likewise, if the bunkroom layout forces all participants into shared rooms against the policy, the site is unsuitable.

When a venue meets the numeric requirements, send a written confirmation request that includes the agreed‑upon sharing rules and asks the venue to acknowledge they can honor them. Keep the response on file as part of the venue evaluation record. This final written step locks in the match between policy, preferences, and physical space before any contract is signed.

The immediate action for the planner is to draft a sharing‑policy memo, circulate it for written approval, and launch a short email survey to capture each participant’s sleeping preference. Once the data are compiled, the planner can apply the baseline budget check and begin requesting quotes that include floor plans, a direct phone number, and a dedicated meeting space.

By completing these steps today, the planner will have a vetted list of venues that already satisfies retreat room assignments shared rooms, eliminating the last‑minute surprise that often derails group retreats. The process creates a transparent, written trail that protects both budget and participant comfort throughout the planning cycle.
