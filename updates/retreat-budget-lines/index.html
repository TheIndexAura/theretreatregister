Retreat Budget Line Items: Add the Rows First-Time Planners Forget

September 4, 2026 · The Retreat Register

A first retreat budget usually holds two rows, lodging and meals. The rest of the spend turns up later, in rows for ground transport, gratuities, session supplies, insurance and damage deposits. Add those rows and a contingency line before any contract is signed, and get the total approved in writing.

The rows that get missed

Ground transport is the first one. Somebody has to move the group from the airport or the train station to the venue, and back again at the end. Unless the venue includes a shuttle in its quote, give that its own row with a note on how the group is arriving.

Gratuities come next. Some venue contracts add a service charge to food and drink, and some leave tipping to you. Read the contract before you decide whether this row is empty, and ask the venue in writing which staff are covered by the service charge.

Ask whoever runs the program what the sessions need. Facilitators often bring handouts, flip charts, markers or props, and someone pays for the printing and the shipping. One email to the facilitator settles this row before the quote is signed.

Insurance gets its own row too. The venue’s policy covers the venue, and the group may need its own; the event insurance guide covers when that is worth buying. Damage deposits belong on the sheet as well. They are usually refundable, but the money leaves your account before the event and comes back after it, so the budget has to carry it in between.

Coffee and snacks outside meal times are the last row people find, usually on the invoice. Ask the venue whether breaks are included in the food-and-beverage package or charged on their own.

Set up the sheet

Give each category its own row and give the sheet three columns: what you expect to pay, what you actually paid, and notes. The notes column holds the vendor contact and the contract reference for that row.

Fill the expected column from written quotes wherever you have one. Where you do not, write “quote requested” and the date you asked. Never leave a cell blank. A row with no cost gets the word “none”, so nobody later wonders whether it was forgotten or free.

The fee questions that decide the venue rows are in the five questions to ask before you book. Ask them before you fill in lodging and meals, because the nightly rate is rarely the number that ends up on the invoice.

Add the contingency and get it approved

Add one row labeled contingency. Its size is a decision for whoever signs off on the budget, and it is easier to agree on a number before anything goes wrong than after. Put the figure they agree to in the expected column.

Then send the whole sheet to the people who approve spending, and ask for the approval in writing. One line does it: “Please confirm you approve this budget, including the contingency row, before I sign the venue contract.”

From then on, the approved sheet is the budget. A later change goes in as a new line with a short reason beside it, and you check the running total against the approved one each time.

A worked example

A planner books a conference center for a two-night team retreat. The venue quote goes into the lodging and meals rows, the facilitator’s supply list goes into its own row, and the shuttle company’s written quote fills the ground transport line.

When the final invoice arrives, the planner copies each amount into the actual column. The caterer has added a charge for a late coffee service that nobody listed, so it goes in as a new line under coffee and snacks, with the invoice reference in the notes. The difference between the two columns shows exactly where the money went.

If the total stays inside the contingency, the job is done. If it does not, the planner writes down why and asks for approval of the extra amount before the next payment goes out.

Rules for the changes nobody planned

If the venue closes on you before the event, the money question comes first, and the contract answers it. Read the cancellation terms, work out what comes back to you and when, and then look at alternatives with that number in hand, and you will spend less time admiring a second venue you cannot afford.

If weather closes the road the shuttle was booked on, add a row for the replacement transport the same day you book it. Take the money from the contingency first and from optional rows such as printing after that. Extra printing has never once saved a retreat.

For anything else that turns up, ask whether it serves the reason the group is meeting. If it does, it can come out of the contingency. If it does not, cut something optional or ask the vendor to lower the charge, and write down which one you did. The printed sheet clipped to the front of the contract folder is the place to write it.
