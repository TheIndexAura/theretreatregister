How to Verify a Venue Is Actually Accessible Before You Book

August 13, 2026 · The Retreat Register

A venue website often lists 'accessible' as a feature. This term, however, can cover a wide range of actual provisions, from a full setup to one ramp and a measure of hope.

What one property considers full access, another might interpret as simply having a single ramp. Discovering the true situation only upon arrival is too late for any group member relying on specific accommodations.

To avoid misunderstandings, a planner must move beyond general terms. When sending written inquiries, ask direct questions about the path from parking to the sleeping quarters and meeting rooms. Inquire about step-free routes, door widths, and the presence of grab bars or roll-in showers.

Treat any vague response as an indication that the required setup is not present. Remember that pricing in this industry is quote-only by default, so specific needs must be clear from the start of any written conversation. A direct phone number and a dedicated meeting or lodging space are the two essentials that most often decide whether a retreat venue works for a group, but the details of accessibility define the experience for some attendees.

The task is to verify true accessibility, not merely accept a checkbox. Draft a precise list of accessibility questions before sending inquiries to any potential venue. Today, begin writing down every specific accessibility requirement your group might have, no matter how small it may seem.
