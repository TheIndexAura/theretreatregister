Measure accessibility at a retreat venue with ADA numbers

August 13, 2026 · The Retreat Register

A venue website says 'accessible' and leaves you to guess. The word can cover a fully fitted property or one ramp and a measure of hope.

What one property calls full access, another calls a single ramp. Finding out which one you booked when the group arrives is too late for anyone who depends on those details.

General terms will not settle it. Ask pointed questions in writing. Start with the path from parking to the sleeping rooms and to the meeting rooms, and ask whether the whole route is step-free. Ask about door widths. Then ask what fittings the bathrooms have, such as grab bars or a roll-in shower.

Read a vague reply as a no. Pricing comes by quote, so put these needs in the first message instead of waiting until the quote is in hand.

Before you write to any venue, list every access requirement your group might have, however small it seems.

Numbers worth putting in the email

The 2010 ADA Standards, posted by the Department of Justice, give measurable figures. A doorway needs a clear opening of 32 inches. An accessible route is 36 inches wide at minimum, though it may narrow to 32 inches for no more than 24 inches of length. A ramp run has a running slope no steeper than 1:12. Ask the venue to state these as measurements, in inches. A plain yes tells you little (U.S. Department of Justice, 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design).

Older buildings sit differently

The Standards cover newly built buildings and the altered parts of existing ones. For existing sites, the ramp rule has an exception that allows steeper slopes within limits set in a table. So an old farmhouse or lodge may not match the figures above, and the only way to learn is to ask for its measurements (2010 ADA Standards, sections 201 and 405).

The roll-in shower question

Table 224.2 sets the minimum number of guest rooms with mobility features. A property with 1 to 25 guest rooms needs one. A property with 26 to 50 needs two. Neither band requires a room with a roll-in shower, and the first one appears at 51 to 75 rooms. A small lodge can meet the table and still have no roll-in shower. Ask for the shower type by room, in writing (2010 ADA Standards, Table 224.2).

Turn the answers into a record

Keep one line per answer: door width, route width, shower type, and the room it applies to. Send the sheet to the attendee who relies on these details and ask whether it covers what they need. A venue that answers in numbers is easy to compare with the next one (U.S. Department of Justice, 2010 ADA Standards).
