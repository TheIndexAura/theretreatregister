From the Data

The direct phone test

July 15, 2026 · The Retreat Register

You have found a promising retreat venue online, and then you spend a good part of the afternoon battling a chatbot or filling in a form that vanishes into the ether. It is a common frustration and a real waste of a planner’s time.

The digital hide-and-seek makes you wonder whether anyone actually works there. Getting a direct answer, or even a direct line, seems to be a lost art in some corners of the industry.

The number that would not move

Between July 12 and July 18, 2026 the register grew from 1,374 venues to 2,281, and was then cut back to 2,125. Across every one of those readings, the share of venues carrying a direct phone number stayed within half a point of 91%.

The sample nearly doubled. Seven hundred venues were added, then a hundred and fifty-six were taken away in a single quality pass. Whole states appeared that had barely been covered before. Through all of it the phone share moved by about a third of a percentage point, from 90.4% to 90.8%.

That is the finding worth your attention, and it is not the same finding as “91% of our venues list a phone number.”

A ratio that holds still while the sample doubles is telling you something about the industry, not about the register. If the 91% were an artefact of a small or lopsided starting set, it would have drifted the moment the set stopped being small and lopsided. It did not drift. Adding six hundred rescued properties did not move it. Adding a fixed brand-family sweep, which unhid whole resort markets, did not move it. Cutting a hundred and fifty-six venues off the list in one day did not move it either.

Roughly nine out of ten American retreat venues put a phone number where a stranger can find it. That has been true of every sample the register has measured, at every size it has been.

The price share fell as the register grew, from 9.7% down to 8.2%, and it never once reached one in ten. The count of venues publishing a price barely moved at all, from 133 to 188, while the register itself gained more than nine hundred venues. Almost every venue added during that week was quote-only.

Put the two tables next to each other and you have the shape of the whole industry in one picture. Venues are willing to be reached. Venues are not willing to be priced.

What “a direct phone number” means here, and what it does not

A direct phone number, in the register’s data, means a telephone number published on the venue’s own website that a caller can dial. That is the whole test.

It does not mean the number reaches a sales office rather than a front desk. It does not mean somebody answers. It does not mean the person who answers can quote a group rate. The register has never measured any of those, and this page is not claiming them.

What the register does claim, in its own approved words, is narrow and checkable. The Retreat Register screens for essentials before listing a venue, focusing first on a direct phone number and dedicated meeting or lodging space.

That is a baseline check on the thing you need first. A venue you cannot contact is not much of a venue.

One correction, since we are showing our arithmetic

An older page on this site, Five questions before you book, gives the July 13, 2026 reading as 91% and prints the two counts behind it, 1,883 of 2,082.2 Those counts divide out to 90.4%, which rounds to 90%, not 91%.

The counts are right and the percentage on that page is a rounding error. We are leaving both here rather than quietly fixing one, because a register that publishes its numbers has to publish the days its numbers disagree. Every other reading in the table above rounds to 91% honestly.

How a planner uses this

Reachability is the cheapest first-pass filter you have, and it costs one phone call.

Call before you email. The register’s data says roughly nine in ten venues give you the option. Skipping the form and dialling the number takes a two-day email round trip down to four minutes, and it tells you immediately whether the property has anyone assigned to group business.

Ask who you are speaking to. Front desk and group sales are different jobs. If the number on the site lands you at the front desk and there is no way through to a sales contact, that is worth knowing in week one rather than week six.

Treat a missing number as information, not a dealbreaker. About one venue in ten does not publish one. Some of those are small, owner-run properties that handle enquiries by email and run perfectly good retreats. What a missing number tells you is that your first contact will be slower, so start earlier with them.

Ask for the price levers on the same call, because the price itself will not be published. Fewer than one venue in ten prints a starting rate. The register’s guidance on that is to “ask the four levers up front: your date flexibility, the buyout threshold, food-and-beverage minimums, and service fees. Those four determine the real number, not the nightly rate.”8

Use the call to test the thing that got 2,193 venues rejected. When the register re-judged its rejection pile on July 12, 2026, the 2,193 that stayed out had no group evidence anywhere on their website.9 A venue that cannot describe its meeting space and overnight capacity on the phone, in the first two minutes, is usually a venue that has never had to.

Where the register’s coverage was on this date

As of July 15, 2026, the register held 2,210 curated retreat venues, with 10 new ones cleared just since the last update. Coverage that day ran deepest in California with 154 venues, New York with 100, Louisiana with 91, and Texas with 86.4

Those four counts belong to July 15, 2026 and to no other date. Two days later Louisiana had 92 and California had 168.6 The day after that, both were smaller again.7 State counts in a register this young are a photograph, not a fact, which is why every one on this site carries the day it was taken.

The honest limits of this page

The last dated phone pair the register published is 1,928 of 2,125, on July 18, 2026.7 There is no newer measurement, and nothing on the site re-checks a venue’s phone number on a schedule. The seven readings above are seven photographs taken on seven days in July 2026.

What makes them worth publishing is not that they are current. It is that there are seven of them, taken while the underlying set of venues changed by more than sixty percent, and the answer barely moved.
