Select a glamping venue that fits a group retreat

August 19, 2026 · The Retreat Register

The tents look incredible at golden hour. Then it rains on night one, and the group finds there is nowhere indoors to gather.

Glamping works for groups when someone asked the unglamorous questions first.

Four questions sort the venues fast. How many bathrooms are there per guest, and how long is the walk? Where does the group gather when the weather wins? Is there heat or power when your season needs it? How far apart are the units, a tight camp or a scatter across a field?

A venue used to groups should answer quickly and plainly. A slow or vague reply may mean the camp was laid out for couples. Either reply tells you what you are booking.

The stars come with the tent. Get the bathroom count in writing before you pay for them.

Ask which building holds the group

The National Weather Service names two safe places in a thunderstorm: a substantial building with electricity or plumbing, and an enclosed vehicle with a metal top. Tents are not on that list (Lightning Safety Tips and Resources). So ask the glamping venue which building holds the whole group, and ask how people reach it from the farthest unit. The piece on the indoor twin for every outdoor block covers the agenda side.

The answer shapes the walk at night, too. A group scattered over a field needs a longer warning than a camp built around one hall. Ask for the distance in writing (Weather Service shelter guidance).

Ask what heats each unit

Cold-season glamping brings a heat question. The EPA lists unvented kerosene and gas space heaters, gas stoves and generators among the sources of carbon monoxide, a gas with no color and almost no smell (US EPA, What is carbon monoxide?). At low levels it causes fatigue. Higher levels bring headaches, dizziness, confusion and nausea, which can pass for a flu.

A heated unit can be a good one, and the heat question has a right answer you can ask for. What heats each unit? Is the heater vented? Where does the generator sit relative to the sleeping units? Send all three in one email and keep the reply with your booking papers (EPA sources of carbon monoxide).
