Skip near me: a retreat search for teams across time zones

July 27, 2026 · The Retreat Register

"Retreat centers near me" works if your whole team lives in one town. For a spread-out team, near me means near your couch.

Say half your crew sits in Denver and the other half in Boston. A local search serves whoever typed it and nobody else. The best venue for that team probably sits near no one's office, and that is fine.

Pick an airport first. Find a major hub that most of the team can reach without a connection fight. Then draw a drive radius around it, an hour or two. That circle is your search area.

Browse venues by region inside that circle, whatever the search engine thinks is closest to your browser. Because prices are quote-only, you will be collecting quotes, so keep the circle tight enough to read through them all.

What counts as a major hub

"Major hub" has a federal meaning. The FAA calls an airport a large hub when it receives 1 percent or more of annual U.S. commercial enplanements (passengers boarding), and a medium hub when it receives 0.25 to 1.0 percent, in its airport categories page. Its CY2025 enplanement data lists Denver International and Boston Logan as large hubs. Both halves of that example team already fly from one.

Choosing the meeting airport

Use the FAA table as a starting list. Pick two or three candidate airports, then look up each home city's flights to them and write the arrival times in a spreadsheet. The goal is simple: nobody lands after your first dinner. The same table gives the three-letter code for each airport.

Put the airport in the email

Once you have a meeting airport, name it in your first written message to a venue, using its code from the FAA table. Ask whether the venue offers a shuttle from that airport and when the last pickup runs. Those two questions sort a short list quickly.
